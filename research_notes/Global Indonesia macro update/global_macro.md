# Global Macro and Markets — 3–7 Day Update (as of 11 October 2026, Asia/Jakarta)

## What changed in the last few days versus prior consensus?

### Takeaway
The fresh macro impulse was stagflationary: the EIA sharply raised its oil-price path, the IEA moved to accelerate emergency stock releases, and U.S. household inflation expectations rose again. At the same time, the Fed signaled that additional hikes are likely but need not be consecutive—more a slow recalibration than an aggressive new tightening cycle.

### Cited Findings
- **The oil baseline was revised materially higher on 6 October.** The U.S. EIA lifted its forecast for Brent to an average **$105/bbl in 4Q26, $14/bbl above its September forecast**, and raised its 2027 average forecast to **$84 from $74**. It cited Saudi East–West pipeline attacks, continuing inventory withdrawals and extreme diesel tightness. EIA's model inputs were finalized on **1 October**, so the forecast explicitly excludes market events after that date and the G7 announcement of 2 October. — [EIA October 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook, pp. 3–5](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_text.pdf)
- EIA estimated Brent averaged **$114/bbl in September**, up **$23/bbl** from August, and briefly reached **$131/bbl on 15 September** after the Saudi bypass-pipeline disruption. Estimated Middle East crude shut-ins eased to **4.8 mb/d in September** from **5.8 mb/d in August** and **10.9 mb/d at the May peak**, but EIA still assumed **4.5 mb/d** of shut-ins in 4Q26. — [EIA October 2026 STEO, pp. 5–6](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_text.pdf)
- **The policy response also escalated on 7 October.** IEA members supported completing the March emergency-stock release as soon as possible and prioritizing diesel. About **325 million barrels** had been released, another roughly **100 million barrels** of previously pledged stocks could come to market, and members retained about **1.1 billion barrels** of public emergency stocks, including more than **200 million barrels of diesel**. — [IEA statement, 7 October 2026](https://www.iea.org/news/statement-by-iea-executive-director-on-meeting-of-iea-member-governments-on-7-october-2026)
- **Consumer inflation psychology deteriorated on 9 October.** The University of Michigan preliminary October sentiment index fell to **46.3 from 48.1** in September (and **53.6** a year earlier). One-year inflation expectations rose to **4.7% from 4.6%**, while long-run expectations rose to **3.5% from 3.4%**—both the second consecutive monthly increase and the highest since May. — [University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers, preliminary October 2026](https://www.sca.isr.umich.edu/)
- **Fed minutes released 7 October were clearly hawkish.** At the 15–16 September meeting, all participants supported a 25 bp increase to **3.75%–4.00%**; most assessed that another increase would likely be appropriate by year-end. The minutes said inflation had made insufficient recent progress, with energy disruption and AI-related investment contributing to pressures. — [Federal Reserve minutes, 15–16 September 2026 meeting](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20260916.htm)
- **But the pace signal softened the immediate interpretation.** On 8 October Governor Christopher Waller said further hikes would be appropriate if data evolve as expected, but they **“do not need to come at consecutive meetings.”** He reported that, as of 7 October, futures implied an **85% chance of at least one hike by December** but only about a **20% chance of two**. — [Federal Reserve speech, 8 October 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20261008a.htm)

### Inferences
- The week's genuine surprise was not merely that oil remained above $100, but that the **official medium-term oil path shifted upward even as physical shut-ins improved**. The market is pricing infrastructure, shipping and refined-product constraints—not just lost crude volumes.
- The IEA acceleration is an important near-term cap on crude, but prioritizing diesel confirms that the binding constraint has migrated downstream into products. For net-energy importers, including Indonesia, this leaves inflation and subsidy/fiscal exposure vulnerable even if headline Brent stabilizes.
- Fed communication points to a “higher, but not hurried” regime: front-end policy risk remains hawkish, while the long end is being driven increasingly by oil, fiscal supply and term premium. This distinction matters for EM: a pause in October would not necessarily deliver large relief to long-duration local bonds.
- The U.S. consumer data create a distributional split: aggregate activity and equities remain resilient, but lower-income sentiment and inflation expectations are worsening. That makes consumption more fragile to any further energy spike than headline growth alone suggests.

### Gaps
- The EIA forecast was finalized on 1 October and therefore is not a real-time forecast of the 7–9 October oil moves or the IEA acceleration; treat it as evidence of a major baseline revision, not a live fair-value estimate.
- September U.S. CPI had **not yet been released** as of 11 October; BLS schedules it for **14 October 2026 at 8:30 a.m. ET**. — [BLS CPI release calendar](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/cpi.htm)

## What moved global bonds, equities, the dollar, and commodities?

### Takeaway
Oil and fiscal/term-premium fears drove large intraday bond swings, but strong Treasury auction demand showed that buyers re-enter at sufficiently high yields. Equities absorbed the rates shock better than duration-sensitive bonds, leaving an unusual combination of record stock indices and multi-decade-high sovereign yields.

### Cited Findings
- On **7 October**, the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield rose as high as **5.36%** from **5.27%** the prior close, near its highest since 2002, before easing to about **5.28%** after a **$39 billion** 10-year auction. Brent settled at **$100.20**, still far above its pre-war **$72** level. The S&P 500 fell **0.2%**, the Dow **0.7%**, and France's CAC 40 **1.2%**. — [AP market wrap, 7 October 2026](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-rates-oil-earnings-44b75c188756038371cf3112ede9cee8)
- On **8 October**, the 10-year Treasury moved from **5.28%** to **5.35%** and then down to **5.23%**. A **$22 billion** 30-year auction cleared at **5.618%**; the 30-year secondary yield subsequently fell to **5.60% from 5.73%**. The auction's official data showed a **2.54 bid-to-cover ratio**. — [AP market wrap, 8 October 2026](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-bonds-oil-us-6a096d714c874db13632794a32cebaa6); [U.S. Treasury auction result PDF](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/instit/annceresult/press/preanre/2026/R_20261008_3.pdf)
- The equity selloff was narrow and duration-sensitive on 8 October: the S&P 500 fell **0.5%** and Nasdaq **1.3%**, while Nvidia fell **2.9%**, Broadcom **4.3%** and Micron **4.8%**; nevertheless, roughly two-thirds of S&P constituents rose after yields eased. — [AP market wrap, 8 October 2026](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-bonds-oil-us-6a096d714c874db13632794a32cebaa6)
- By the **9 October** close, the S&P 500 had rebounded **0.6%**, the Dow **0.8%**, and Nasdaq **0.6%**. Brent settled at **$104.72** and the 10-year Treasury at **5.24%**. European equity indices gained at least 1% in France, Germany and the UK; Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose **1.8%**. — [AP market wrap, 9 October 2026](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-oil-ai-bonds-5d0f953dbf96febb0690c1aef23fa8a9)
- The fiscal selloff was global, not uniquely American. On 7 October, the French 10-year yield rose **18 bp to 4.93%** amid budget and protest concerns. UK 30-year gilt yields reached **6.036%**, the highest since January 1998. Banque de France data show a French benchmark 10-year-equivalent index near **4.898% on 8 October**, up from **4.488% on 10 September**. — [Axios, 7 October 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/07/france-global-bond-markets); [Reuters, 7 October 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/uk-30year-gilt-yields-hit-28year-high-in-global-selloff-4937055); [Banque de France bond indices, 8 October 2026](https://www.banque-france.fr/en/statistics/rates-and-prices/bond-indexes-2026-10-08)
- In Japan, the 10-year JGB yield retreated toward **3.00% on 9 October** after reaching **3.153%** earlier in the week, around a 30-year high, as markets pushed the expected next BOJ hike from October toward December. — [Reuters, 9 October 2026](https://www.fidelity.com/news/article/us-markets/202610082118RTRSNEWSCOMBINED_L4N45V01T_1); [FXStreet market report, 9 October 2026](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/british-pound-gains-ground-as-japanese-yen-underperforms-202610091220)

### Inferences
- The bond selloff is best read as a **joint oil-plus-fiscal term-premium shock**, not simply a repricing of the next Fed meeting. The fact that yields rose across the U.S., UK, France and Japan, then reversed on strong auctions, suggests duration supply and required compensation are as important as central-bank expectations.
- The auction response is a useful contrarian point: this is severe repricing, but not yet a buyers' strike. Demand returned at 5.3%–5.6% U.S. yields. That can stabilize markets tactically without resolving the structural debt and inflation problem.
- Equities' resilience is increasingly concentrated and conditional. Broad participation improved when yields fell, but AI/semiconductor duration proxies sold off sharply even on evidence of strong sector revenue. A renewed long-end breakout would challenge the “equities can ignore bonds” narrative.
- A precise dollar index move was not included because primary-source real-time FX data were not available in the research set. Directionally, high U.S. yields are dollar-supportive, but the 8 October intraday decline in yields interrupted that channel; avoid claiming a clean one-way USD move this week.

### Gaps
- Cross-market closes differ by timezone and data vendor; the figures above use dated AP/Reuters market reports and official auction data. They should not be mixed as one synchronized snapshot.
- No reliable primary-source real-time USD index close was located within the research window.

## Which developments have second-order implications for emerging markets and Indonesia?

### Takeaway
Indonesia faces a mixed shock: high energy prices can support commodity export receipts, but refined-product scarcity, higher global yields and a less-dovish Fed increase the burden on inflation management, the fiscal/subsidy bill, the rupiah and local bond funding. The most relevant transmission may be diesel and global duration rather than Brent alone.

### Cited Findings
- The IEA's decision to prioritize diesel reflects current refined-product tightness; it reported over **200 million barrels of diesel** in member emergency stocks and another roughly **100 million barrels** of pledged oil stocks that could still be released. — [IEA, 7 October 2026](https://www.iea.org/news/statement-by-iea-executive-director-on-meeting-of-iea-member-governments-on-7-october-2026)
- EIA forecast U.S. East Coast distillate inventories at **32% below** their five-year seasonal average in September and around **20% below** average through the coming winter; it cited global diesel tightness as an independent source of upward crude-price pressure. — [EIA October 2026 STEO, p. 3](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_text.pdf)
- The IMF said on 1 October that rising advanced- and emerging-market yields reflected energy-driven policy expectations, high public debt and higher term premia; EM spread compression had partly offset the rise in benchmark yields, and markets were still functioning in an orderly fashion. It also noted refined-product prices for diesel, gasoline and jet fuel were **60%–97% above pre-conflict levels**. — [IMF press briefing transcript, 1 October 2026](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/10/01/tr-10012026-imf-press-briefing-transcript-october-01-2026)
- China's September official manufacturing PMI returned to expansion at **50.1 from 49.8**, while non-manufacturing activity recovered to **50.2 from 49.0**. However, small firms remained in contraction at **48.9**, employment was **48.4**, and non-manufacturing new orders were only **46.5**. The data were released on 30 September and the English page was posted on 8 October, so this is context rather than a true 3–7 day event. — [China NBS September 2026 PMI](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202610/t20261008_1965466.html)

### Inferences
- **Indonesia's key near-term risk is a three-leg transmission:** expensive imported fuels/refined products → administered-price/subsidy pressure; higher U.S. and global term premia → pressure on SBN yields and foreign demand; and higher-for-longer Fed expectations → less room for Bank Indonesia to ease without weakening the rupiah.
- Higher crude and coal-related revenues may improve parts of the trade balance, but diesel tightness can still worsen domestic distribution, transport and food costs. The net effect is less favorable than a simple “Indonesia is a commodity exporter” framing suggests.
- China's return to only marginal expansion provides some support for Indonesian metals, coal and intermediate-goods demand, but weak small-firm employment and sub-50 service new orders argue against assuming a broad China demand boom.
- Watch whether EM credit spreads continue to compress enough to offset rising risk-free yields. If not, even fundamentally stronger EMs could face tightening financial conditions without a domestic deterioration.

### Gaps
- This global workstream did not independently collect Indonesia-specific daily rupiah, SBN, JCI, BI intervention or subsidy data; those should be combined with the Indonesia-focused research before finalizing portfolio implications.
- The China PMI event date was 30 September, outside the strict 3–7 day window; it is included only because the English official release became available on 8 October and because China is a major Indonesia transmission channel.

## What is the most interesting non-obvious debate or contrarian angle?

### Takeaway
The most useful huddle debate is whether markets face a classic monetary-tightening problem or a **market-structure problem**: leveraged hedge funds now intermediate a meaningful share of sovereign debt just as fiscal supply, oil volatility and AI concentration are rising. High yields may attract final buyers, but the path to equilibrium can still be disorderly if leveraged positions unwind.

### Cited Findings
- On **6 October**, the IMF reported that hedge-fund gross assets under management had tripled since 2013 to about **$13 trillion**, gross notional exposures were around **$40 trillion**, and hedge funds held about **9% of U.S. Treasuries in 2025**. The IMF highlighted leverage, crowded positions and redemption pressures as mechanisms that can amplify market stress. — [IMF, “Hedge Funds Improve Market Functioning, but Can Also Amplify Stress,” 6 October 2026](https://www.imf.org/en/blogs/articles/2026/10/06/hedge-funds-improve-market-functioning-but-can-also-amplify-stress)
- The Fed minutes themselves noted that Treasury markets were functioning smoothly but that policymakers should plan for market dysfunction and strengthen strategy, communication and tools for stress while limiting the Fed's market footprint. — [Federal Reserve minutes, released 7 October 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20260916.htm)
- The IMF's April work on EM capital flows found that leveraged hedge-fund strategies can trigger margin-driven forced sales during volatility spikes and create outsized price effects in shallower EM markets, although less-leveraged funds can also provide liquidity and stabilize markets. — [IMF GFSR, April 2026, Chapter 2](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/abstract/book/9798229035910/CH002.xml)

### Inferences
- **Contrarian bull case:** auctions show that 5%+ sovereign yields are becoming self-stabilizing by pulling in real-money demand; oil stock releases can bridge the supply gap; equities can remain resilient if earnings growth offsets discount-rate pressure.
- **Contrarian bear case:** the same auctions that appear reassuring may mask a growing reliance on leveraged intermediaries. An oil spike, fiscal shock or AI-equity correction could force simultaneous deleveraging across Treasuries, equities and EM assets—turning a slow term-premium repricing into a liquidity event.
- For Indonesia, the market-structure angle matters because local assets can be sold as liquid proxies during a global deleveraging episode even when domestic fundamentals have not changed. This argues for monitoring foreign positioning, FX liquidity and cross-asset correlations—not only BI policy and local data.
- **Suggested huddle question:** “Are 5%–6% developed-market sovereign yields the cure because they attract buyers, or the trigger because leveraged intermediaries cannot warehouse the volatility?”
