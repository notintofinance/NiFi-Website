# Indonesia macro and markets update — information available through 11 October 2026 (Jakarta time)

## What changed in the last few days, and why does it matter?

### Takeaway
The new information mix is stagflationary at the margin: domestic demand is still expanding, but only slowly, while the oil shock is already showing up in the subsidy bill and the rupiah remains close to Rp17,900/US$. That combination makes an October BI rate cut very unlikely and raises the value of fiscal/FX hedges even though headline inflation remains inside target.

### Cited Findings
- **Fiscal data released 9 October (covering January–September):** revenue reached Rp2,341.5tn, 74.2% of the 2026 target, and spending Rp2,660.5tn, 69.2% of budget and 19% higher year on year. The resulting deficit was Rp319tn, or 1.24% of GDP, versus the full-year budget design of Rp689.1tn/2.68% of GDP. Tax receipts were Rp1,607.6tn, up 24.1% year on year. — [Ministry of Finance, 9 Oct](https://wip.kemenkeu.go.id/informasi-publik/publikasi/berita-utama/Menkeu-Suahasil-APBN-KiTA-September-2026); [Ministry of Finance press release, 9 Oct](https://www.kemenkeu.go.id/informasi-publik/publikasi/siaran-pers/apbn-menumbuhkan-apbn-melindungi)
- **The oil shock is already fiscal, not merely a market story:** energy subsidy and compensation payments had reached Rp376.8tn by September, up 54.1% year on year and equal to 84.3% of the full-year target; the finance minister linked the increase to ICP, the rupiah and higher subsidised fuel/LPG/electricity volumes. — [ANTARA, event/publication 9 Oct](https://en.antaranews.com/amp/news/434854/energy-subsidies-compensation-reach-843-pct-of-2026-target-minister)
- Brent was around US$104/bbl in the 9 October market brief, while Indonesia's September ICP was reported at US$91.9/bbl; both are well above the 2026 budget's US$70/bbl oil-price assumption. — [Samuel Sekuritas, 9 Oct](https://samuel.co.id/research-reports/samuel-daily-research-9-october-2026/); [Jawa Pos quoting the finance minister, 9 Oct](https://www.jawapos.com/finance/2610090275/harga-minyak-tembus-usd-105-per-barel-menkeu-wanti-wanti-subsidi-energi-naik?page=all); [Ministry of Finance budget assumptions](https://fiskal.kemenkeu.go.id/files/red/file/1759288377_v2_red_september_2025_-_01.pdf)
- **Household demand is positive but not strong:** BI's 8 October release put September consumer confidence at 118.1; expectations improved to 128.7 from 127.6, but the current-conditions index slipped to 107.5 from 109.4. — [Bank Indonesia, survey period September; release 8 Oct](https://www.bi.go.id/en/publikasi/ruang-media/news-release/Pages/sp_2821626.aspx)
- BI's 9 October retail survey estimated September real retail sales growth at 1.4% year on year, only slightly above August's realised 1.3%; retailers expect November and February price-pressure indices of 149.9 and 177.9, respectively, up from 146.5 and 166.0 in the prior survey. — [Bank Indonesia, release 9 Oct](https://www.bi.go.id/en/publikasi/ruang-media/news-release/Pages/sp_2821726.aspx)
- The latest CPI release (1 October, covering September) showed headline inflation at 3.28% year on year and 0.30% month on month, with core at 2.84% year on year. Headline is still inside BI's 2.5%±1 percentage-point target but close to its 3.5% ceiling. — [BPS, release 1 Oct](https://www.bps.go.id/en/pressrelease/2026/10/01/2623/the-year-on-year--y-on-y--headline-inflation-in-september-2026-was-recorded-at-3-28-percent-.html); [BI September policy statement](https://www.bi.go.id/id/publikasi/ruang-media/news-release/Pages/sp_2819326.aspx)
- September manufacturing PMI rebounded to 52.4 from 49.8 in August, according to the Finance Ministry's 9 October macro update, adding evidence that real activity has not rolled over despite the tighter financial setting. — [Ministry of Finance, 9 Oct](https://www.kemenkeu.go.id/informasi-publik/publikasi/siaran-pers/apbn-menumbuhkan-apbn-melindungi)

### Inferences
- The fiscal position looks comfortable in the headline deficit, but that understates the marginal problem: 84.3% of the full-year energy-support allocation was already used after nine months, leaving little buffer if oil stays near US$100 and the government avoids retail fuel-price increases.
- The demand surveys argue against a hard-landing narrative, but they also do not support a consumption boom: the forward-looking confidence measure improved while current conditions softened and retail growth remained near 1%–1.5%.

### Gaps
- The October APBN release did not provide, in the accessible text, a revised full-year subsidy forecast under a sustained US$100-plus Brent scenario.

## How did the rupiah, SBN, JCI, foreign flows and commodity-linked sectors react?

### Takeaway
Indonesia traded as a high-carry but oil-sensitive market: the rupiah and 10-year SBN were broadly stable over the week but at weak absolute levels, foreign investors kept selling equities, and Friday's relief rally was led by basic materials when oil eased.

### Cited Findings
- On 8 October, USD/IDR closed at 17,915, down 0.2% on the day but 0.3% weaker than a week earlier and 7.4% weaker year to date. The 10-year rupiah government yield was 7.198%, 3.1bp higher than a week earlier and 100.3bp higher year to date; the 2-year yield was 6.794%, 0.8bp lower on the week, producing a mild bear-steepening beyond the front end. — [ADB AsianBondsOnline, market close 8 Oct](https://asianbondsonline.adb.org/indonesia/)
- The rupiah strengthened 0.09% on Friday, 9 October, to Rp17,889/US$, alongside a regional FX rebound. Earlier in the week, BRI Danareksa recorded Rp17,907 on 5 October versus Rp17,875 previously, while 10-year SBN yield rose 3.5bp to 7.191% as the US 10-year yield reached 5.31%. — [IDNFinancials, 9 Oct](https://www.idnfinancials.com/news/70071/jci-surges-1-04-as-crude-oil-prices-fall); [BRI Danareksa, 6 Oct note reporting 5 Oct close](https://www.brights.id/id/riset-dan-berita/laporan-riset/brids-daily-economic-fixed-income-update-tuesday-october-6-2026)
- The October 6 sovereign-sukuk auction received Rp32.615tn of bids for Rp10tn awarded, a 3.26x aggregate cover. Weighted-average awarded yields ranged from 6.38% for the November 2026 bill to 7.21525% for PBS038 due December 2049. — [DJPPR/Ministry of Finance, auction event and release 6 Oct](https://djppr.kemenkeu.go.id/en/sovereignshariasecuritiesauctionresultontuesdayoctober6%2C2026)
- The JCI fell 0.75% on 7 October and 1.88% on 8 October, then rebounded 1.04% on 9 October to 6,093.76 after briefly breaking below 6,000. On Friday, 10 of 11 sectors rose; basic materials led at +1.67%, while property was the sole decliner at -0.16%. — [Investing.com daily history](https://au.investing.com/indices/idx-composite-historical-data); [Katadata, 9 Oct close](https://databoks.katadata.co.id/en/market/statistics/6ac8b8ec0f2bc/jci-rises-104-to-the-6093-level-friday-october-9-2026); [IDNFinancials, 9 Oct](https://www.idnfinancials.com/news/70071/jci-surges-1-04-as-crude-oil-prices-fall)
- A 10 October report citing IDX Mobile put the JCI up 0.94% for the trading week and foreign net selling at Rp2.44tn, 51.44% less than the prior week; domestic investors accounted for 77% of transactions. The article's displayed date range says “5 September–9 October,” an apparent typographical error, so use the flow and weekly-change figures with that caveat. — [IDN Times, published 10 Oct](https://www.idntimes.com/business/economy/ihsg-menguat-tipis-pekan-ini-net-sell-asing-menurun-00-3m8tp-lcrbp3)
- The Ministry of Finance said combined net foreign inflows into SBN and BI securities were positive Rp171tn from 1 January through 7 October, showing the divergence between fixed-income inflows and equity outflows. — [Ministry of Finance, statement 8 Oct](https://www.kemenkeu.go.id/informasi-publik/publikasi/siaran-pers/APBN-Tumbuhkan-Perekonomian)
- Friday's equity rebound was attributed to renewed declines in crude amid US–Iran negotiation developments; it coincided with the leadership of basic materials and gains in BBCA (+0.83%) and BBRI (+2.31%). — [IDNFinancials, 9 Oct](https://www.idnfinancials.com/news/70071/jci-surges-1-04-as-crude-oil-prices-fall)

### Inferences
- Foreign positioning is bifurcated rather than uniformly risk-off: investors continue to harvest high SBN/SRBI carry, while avoiding equities. That supports the rupiah at the margin but leaves the JCI dependent on local liquidity and commodity headlines.
- The curve move is more consistent with global duration/oil/fiscal-risk repricing than imminent BI easing: the 2-year outperformed while 5- and 10-year yields rose on the week.
- The basic-materials rally should not be read as “oil is good for Indonesia.” Listed commodity exporters can benefit, but the sovereign is a net oil importer with capped retail prices and an outsized subsidy bill.

### Gaps
- No primary IDX weekly-statistics page was indexed for 5–9 October; the weekly foreign-equity-flow figure above is therefore a secondary report citing IDX Mobile.
- The Ministry of Finance statement aggregates SBN and BI-securities inflows, so it does not reveal the precise split between government bonds and SRBI/SVBI instruments.

## Are there new BI, fiscal, subsidy, trade or industrial-policy signals?

### Takeaway
There was no new policy-rate decision, but the data reinforce a stability-first BI stance. The clearest new policy signal is fiscal: the government is still presenting the budget as on track while energy support absorbs the shock, making subsidy management—not the headline deficit—the key Indonesia policy question.

### Cited Findings
- BI held its policy rate at 5.75% on 23 September after 100bp of hikes during the second quarter, explicitly prioritising rupiah stabilisation and capital inflows; its toolkit includes offshore NDF, domestic spot and DNDF intervention. — [BI policy statement, event/release 23 Sep](https://www.bi.go.id/id/publikasi/ruang-media/news-release/Pages/sp_2819326.aspx); [BI Monetary Policy Review, published 29 Sep](https://www.bi.go.id/en/publikasi/laporan/Pages/TKM-September-2026.aspx)
- Official reserves were US$146.3bn at end-September, down only US$0.2bn from August and equal to 5.3 months of imports (5.2 months including government external-debt service). BI said the movement reflected government external borrowing and tax/services receipts against debt repayments and rupiah-stabilisation operations. — [Bank Indonesia, release 7 Oct](https://www.bi.go.id/id/iru/highlight-news/Pages/Cadangan-Devisa-September-2026-Tetap-Terjaga.aspx)
- BI's September adjusted base money grew 9.3% year on year to Rp2,353.1tn, slowing from 16.3% in August. — [Bank Indonesia release index, published 7 Oct](https://www.bi.go.id/en/Publikasi/Ruang-Media/news-release/default.aspx)
- The latest BPS trade data (released 1 October, covering August) showed exports of US$26.61bn (+6.72% year on year) and imports of US$23.06bn (+19.09%), for a US$3.55bn monthly surplus. January–August exports rose 4.74% to US$193.64bn, but imports jumped 19.84% to US$186.39bn, leaving only a US$7.25bn cumulative surplus; the non-oil surplus was US$28.54bn versus a US$21.29bn oil-and-gas deficit. — [BPS, release 1 Oct](https://www.bps.go.id/en/pressrelease/2026/10/01/2621/exports-and-imports-of-indonesia-in-august-2026-reached-usd26-61-billion-and-usd23-06-billion--respectively.html)
- No major new trade or downstreaming/industrial-policy measure was found in the 5–11 October official releases reviewed; the material new government information was fiscal execution and subsidy absorption.

### Inferences
- A near-stable reserve stock despite explicit intervention suggests BI can continue smoothing volatility, but it does not remove the pressure: USD/IDR near 17,900, headline CPI at 3.28%, 5.2%–5.3 months of reserve cover and high oil favour a hold or hike bias, not cuts.
- The August trade surplus is flattering at the margin because import growth is running almost four times export growth year to date. With the oil-and-gas deficit already US$21.29bn before the latest Brent spike, the external balance is more vulnerable than the monthly US$3.55bn surplus suggests.

### Gaps
- BI has not issued a formal October policy-rate signal since the latest oil move; the next decision is scheduled for 20–21 October.

## What forward calendar and risks matter over the coming week?

### Takeaway
The coming week is light on top-tier domestic releases, so global oil, US yields/dollar and portfolio flows should dominate price action; Indonesia's August external-debt release is the main scheduled domestic data point. The October BI decision follows on 20–21 October.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Watch three high-frequency thresholds for the huddle: Brent around/above US$100, USD/IDR around 18,000, and the 10-year SBN above 7.20%. A simultaneous breach would raise the probability that BI needs tighter SRBI pricing, stronger intervention or a rate hike at the 20–21 October meeting.
- The external-debt release matters less for the level than for composition and refinancing: reserves cover looks adequate, but high US yields raise the cost of rolling external and sovereign debt.

### Gaps

## What is the most interesting non-consensus Indonesia angle?

### Takeaway
**The huddle-worthy angle: Indonesia's apparent commodity hedge is breaking down.** Coal, metals and palm-oil equities can still rally on a commodity upswing, but oil above US$100 simultaneously worsens the trade balance, subsidy bill, inflation risk, rupiah and SBN term premium. At the sovereign level, Indonesia is increasingly an oil-import shock story, not simply a commodity winner.

### Cited Findings
- The oil-and-gas trade deficit was already US$21.29bn in January–August, while total imports rose 19.84% year on year, versus only 4.74% export growth. — [BPS, released 1 Oct](https://www.bps.go.id/en/pressrelease/2026/10/01/2621/exports-and-imports-of-indonesia-in-august-2026-reached-usd26-61-billion-and-usd23-06-billion--respectively.html)
- Energy subsidy/compensation spending reached Rp376.8tn by September, 84.3% of the annual target and 54.1% above a year earlier, as the rupiah weakened and energy prices/volumes rose. — [ANTARA, 9 Oct](https://en.antaranews.com/amp/news/434854/energy-subsidies-compensation-reach-843-pct-of-2026-target-minister)
- The rupiah was 7.4% weaker year to date and the 10-year SBN yield 100.3bp higher year to date on 8 October, even though combined foreign inflows into SBN and BI securities were positive Rp171tn through 7 October. — [ADB AsianBondsOnline, 8 Oct close](https://asianbondsonline.adb.org/indonesia/); [Ministry of Finance, 8 Oct statement](https://www.kemenkeu.go.id/informasi-publik/publikasi/siaran-pers/APBN-Tumbuhkan-Perekonomian)
- At the same time, the JCI's 9 October rebound was led by basic materials (+1.67%), illustrating why equity-sector performance can send the opposite message from the sovereign macro impulse. — [IDNFinancials, 9 Oct](https://www.idnfinancials.com/news/70071/jci-surges-1-04-as-crude-oil-prices-fall)

### Inferences
- The most useful relative-value framing is **long selected non-oil commodity exporters versus cautious banks/property/duration**, rather than a blanket “long Indonesia commodity beta.” Banks face the second-round risk from tighter BI policy and softer purchasing power; property was already the only sector down in Friday's broad rally.
- The headline fiscal deficit of 1.24% of GDP is a lagging comfort indicator. The leading constraint is the subsidy run-rate: if prices are administratively capped, the adjustment is displaced into borrowing, spending cuts/reallocation or SOE compensation arrears.
- Because foreign fixed-income inflows remain positive, the first sign of a sharper regime change would be not equity selling—which is already occurring—but a reversal in SBN/SRBI inflows alongside USD/IDR testing 18,000.

### Gaps
- A full fiscal sensitivity estimate requires the government's current 2026 pass-through assumptions for ICP, USD/IDR and subsidised volumes; the latest public release provides realised spending but not those elasticities.
