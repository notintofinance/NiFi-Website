# Mainland China conventional banking: scale, growth, customers and fintech interaction, 2010–2026 YTD

## How fast did conventional banks grow in assets, loans, deposits, profits and digital users?

### Takeaway

China's conventional banking system remained the economy's dominant balance-sheet intermediary throughout 2010–2026. Total banking assets rose from RMB95.3tn at end-2010 to RMB480tn at end-2025, an 11.4% compound annual growth rate (CAGR), but the five-year CAGR decelerated from 15.9% in 2010–2015 to 8.5% in 2020–2025. Deposits and loans followed the same pattern: 2010–2025 CAGRs of 10.7% and 11.9%, respectively, with much slower growth after 2020. Commercial-bank profit expanded far more slowly than the balance sheet after 2015, while return on equity (ROE), return on assets (ROA) and net interest margin (NIM) compressed. The industry's response was not retreat but scale plus digitisation: hundreds of millions of customers now use bank apps, and 93.86% of bank transactions were already off-counter in 2023. The 2026 observations below are actual H1 or latest reported figures, never full-year actuals.

### Inferences

- Balance-sheet growth has become structurally less profitable. Assets grew roughly five percentage points per year faster than profit over 2010–2025, while reported ROE more than halved. This inference follows the milestone CAGR and return series above; it should not be read as a single-factor claim because capital accumulation, mix, provisioning, policy pricing and rate cycles all contributed.
- The state-controlled large-bank group gained asset share after 2020 despite fintech competition. That suggests fintech captured customer attention and high-frequency activity without displacing banks as funded balance-sheet owners. It may also reflect policy-directed credit, deposit-flight-to-quality and consolidation; the regulatory totals alone cannot apportion causality.
- “Users” should never be summed across banks. A Chinese adult commonly has accounts/apps at multiple banks, and registered users, customers with assets, monthly active users (MAU), accounts and cards are different denominators. MAU is the cleanest engagement measure in bank disclosures, but definitions remain bank-specific.

### Gaps

- A consistent annual 2010–2025 sector panel for NIM, fee income, cost/income, CET1 and provision coverage is not exposed in one regulator download. Bloomberg plus Wind/CEIC would materially improve the annual continuity.
- Full-year 2026 is unavailable by definition. Use NFRA 2026 Q2 and bank 2026 interim results as actuals, and put all 2026E estimates in a visibly separate forecast column.
- CCB's 2025 press release did not reproduce deposits and loans; obtain these from the full audited annual report or Bloomberg before a company model is published.
- Bank customer and app metrics need hand collection from annual reports because data vendors often omit MAU, retail AUM and wealth-client definitions.

## What customer jobs are conventional banks used for?

### Takeaway

The conventional bank remains the customer's regulated financial “system of record”: deposit and salary custody, bank settlement accounts, cards, mortgages and other long-duration consumer credit, corporate working capital and project finance, rural and inclusive-SME lending, cross-border settlement, custody, investment/insurance distribution and wealth management. Fintech firms often own discovery, QR acceptance, daily wallet engagement, comparison and embedded lending, but most balances and regulated products ultimately sit with, are funded by, or clear through banks.

### Cited Findings

**Deposits and payments/custody.** At end-2025, all financial institutions held RMB336.1tn of deposits, including RMB167.0tn of household deposits; outstanding loans were RMB275.7tn ([NBS 2025 communiqué, Financial Intermediation section](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202602/t20260228_1962661.html)). At end-2024, banks maintained 14.951bn settlement accounts, of which 14.840bn were individual accounts; they had issued 9.913bn bank cards, including 9.186bn debit and 727m credit cards ([PBOC, *Overall Operation of the Payment System in 2024*, pp. 1–5](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/en/3688247/3688978/3709143/2025080817520765198/2025040114593718714.pdf)). These are accounts/cards, not unique people.

**Consumer and mortgage-like lending.** Consumer loans rose from RMB49.57tn in 2020 to RMB58.2tn in 2025. The 2025 total comprised RMB48.7tn of medium/long-term consumer lending and RMB9.5tn short-term ([NBS 2020 communiqué](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202102/t20210228_1814177.html); [NBS 2025 communiqué](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202602/t20260228_1962661.html)). The official category includes mortgages and other consumer credit, so it should not be labelled “mortgages” without a separate PBOC mortgage series.

**SME, rural and policy-aligned credit.** Inclusive micro- and small-enterprise (MSE) loans were RMB21.5tn in 2020, RMB33.3tn in 2024 (+14.7% YoY) and RMB37.0tn in 2025 (+11% YoY); inclusive agriculture-related lending was RMB14.2tn in 2025 (+10.3%) ([NBS 2020 communiqué](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202102/t20210228_1814177.html); [NFRA 2024 Chinese release](https://www.nfra.gov.cn/cn/view/pages/ItemDetail.html?docId=1199327); [NFRA 2025 indicators](https://www.nfra.gov.cn/en/view/pages/ItemDetail.html?docId=1251267&itemId=980)). ABC illustrates the specialization: at end-2025 it reported RMB10.9tn of county-area loans, RMB4.35tn of inclusive loans, RMB3.93tn of inclusive MSE loans and 5.239m MSE borrowers ([ABC 2025 Annual Report transcript](https://financialfilings.com/filings/agricultural-bank-of-china-limited/annual-report/2026/60236936/)).

**Corporate finance and cross-border settlement.** BOC said it handled roughly 25% of China's cross-border settlement in 2025, and its global custody network covered more than 100 countries and regions ([BOC 2025 results](https://wap.boc.cn/bif/bi1/202603/t20260330_25657386.html)). ICBC had 14.746m corporate customers and 18.94m corporate digital-banking customers, with 8.36m corporate digital MAU, at end-2025 ([ICBC 2025 Annual Report](https://v.icbc.com.cn/userfiles/Resources/ICBCLTD/download/2026/2025AnnualReport.pdf)). These services—cash management, trade finance, FX, guarantees, bond underwriting and custody—are broader than retail fintech's usual payment/credit interface.

**Wealth and investment distribution.** Bank wealth-management product (WMP) balances rose from RMB10.24tn in 2013 to RMB15.02tn in 2014; at end-2024 they were RMB29.95tn, with 125m investors, 30,800 outstanding products, 179 banks and 31 bank wealth-management companies participating. The 2024 products channelled about RMB21tn to the real economy ([Xinhua/CNFIN, 2015-05-28, citing the China Banking Wealth Management Registration System](https://www.cnfin.com/bank-xh08/a/20150528/1504906.shtml); [State Council/Xinhua, 2025-01-17, citing the 2024 bank WMP report](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/statistics/202501/17/content_WS678a376cc6d0868f4e8eee9d.html)). In 2024, large banks accounted for 37.16% of bank WMP balance, joint-stock banks 46.62%, city banks 14.28% and rural banks 1.94% ([EY 2024 listed-bank review, wealth-management section](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-cn/newsroom/2025/5/documents/ey-listed-banks-in-china-2024-review-and-outlook-en.pdf)).

**Bank-level wealth depth.** ICBC reported personal financial assets under management (AUM) of RMB25.37tn and more than 10m WMP holders in 2025 ([ICBC 2025 Annual Report](https://v.icbc.com.cn/userfiles/Resources/ICBCLTD/download/2026/2025AnnualReport.pdf)). ABC reported retail AUM of RMB24.7tn and WMP balance of RMB2.151tn ([ABC 2025 Annual Report transcript](https://financialfilings.com/filings/agricultural-bank-of-china-limited/annual-report/2026/60236936/)). CMB reported retail AUM above RMB17tn in 2025; in 2024 it had 58.216m WMP holders, 10.376m asset-allocation customers and RMB4.48tn of managed assets ([CMB 2025 Annual Report](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0427/2026042700894.pdf); [CMB 2024 Annual Report](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0416/2025041600321.pdf)).

### Inferences

- The bank and fintech “markets” overlap rather than divide cleanly. A wallet transaction can be initiated by a platform but funded from a bank account; a fund can be discovered on a platform but custodied by a bank; a platform can originate a loan while a bank funds or securitises it. Market share must therefore be measured at a named layer—customer interface, payment initiation, clearing, funding, credit risk, deposit custody or product manufacture.
- CMB demonstrates that a conventional bank can win on interface and advice, not just funding. Its 129m mobile MAU, >RMB17tn retail AUM, and retail finance contribution above 50% of both revenue and profit in 2025 support a digitally engaged retail-bank model rather than a simple branch model ([CMB 2025 annual-report transcript](https://financialfilings.com/filings/china-merchants-bank-co-ltd/annual-report/2026/60237223/)).
- The WMP market's roughly doubling from 2014 to 2024 occurred alongside the rise of platform fund distribution. This is coexistence, not evidence that either channel caused the other's growth: platforms expanded discovery and comparison, while bank balance sheets, wealth subsidiaries and customer trust supported product manufacture and custody.

### Gaps

- No public source provides an annual, non-overlapping split of customer intent (transaction vs saving vs borrowing vs investing) across all banks. A defensible view needs bank-app survey data or microdata, not account/card counts.
- Salary-account penetration, mortgage-only balances, fund/insurance distribution sales, private-banking AUM and custody assets should be collected separately. Current categories are too broad for market-share attribution.
- Wealth “AUM” is especially inconsistent: some banks report personal financial assets held, some managed assets, some WMP balances and some sales volumes. Each series must retain its original label.

## How has mobile-banking adoption changed bank economics and client behaviour?

### Takeaway

Mobile banking converted conventional banks from branch-first utilities into high-frequency digital platforms, even as third-party wallets remained the more visible retail-payment interface. The operational shift is unambiguous: registered bank mobile users rose from 72.6m in 2010 to hundreds of millions at each large bank; bank mobile-payment transaction count increased from 13.8bn in 2015 to 211.0bn in 2024; off-counter transactions reached 491.4bn in 2023 with a 93.86% electronic-channel diversion rate. The economic effect is mixed: banks likely lowered marginal service costs and defended customer data/engagement, but public data do not isolate those savings, while NIM and fee pressure continued.

### Inferences

- Client behaviour shifted from occasional branch visits to continuous app engagement. The most defensible evidence is the 93.86% off-counter diversion rate, bank-app MAU and 15-fold increase in bank mobile transaction count. This likely reduced routine teller traffic, while physical networks remained relevant for onboarding, complex advice, cash, elderly customers and rural coverage.
- Mobile did not reverse bank-margin compression. Digitisation can reduce service unit cost, but sector NIM fell sharply in 2020–2025 and public disclosures do not isolate “cost saved per digital transaction.” Claims that apps caused or offset a specific amount of profit should be avoided without management data.
- Banks defended the relationship layer unevenly. CMB's 129m MAU against 224m retail customers implies much stronger disclosed engagement than BOC's >100m MAU against >550m personal customers, but the ratios are not fully comparable because each bank defines customers and MAU differently.

### Gaps

- Obtain annual MAU, digital sales share, app-originated loans, app-originated wealth sales, cost per transaction and branch traffic for each bank. Public reporting is intermittent and definitions change.
- The 2025 full-year PBOC payment-system report should replace Q3 stock figures once available; keep 2025 Q3 labelled as such until then.
- PBOC channel totals do not allocate activity to ICBC/CCB/ABC/BOC/CMB or Alipay/WeChat Pay. Company-level transaction market share requires a separate, transparently defined dataset.

## How did fintech competition affect fees, acquisition, user experience, underwriting and wealth distribution?

### Takeaway

Fintech competition attacked the high-frequency interface rather than the banking balance sheet. Alipay/Tenpay-style ecosystems made QR/mobile payment, instant onboarding, embedded credit and low-minimum investment the expected experience; banks responded with super-apps, remote servicing, data-led credit and wealth ecosystems. Competition reduced the strategic value of bank-card swipe fees and branch access as acquisition moats, while regulation and bank funding made the two sectors interdependent. The strongest empirical conclusion is complementarity with bargaining tension, not wholesale substitution.

### Cited Findings

**Payments and customer acquisition.** BIS research described China's two leading big-tech ecosystems as accounting for about 94% of mobile payments in the study period and highlighted their use of e-commerce and QR-payment data in SME lending ([BIS Working Paper 1037, *Big techs versus banks*, pp. 3–6](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1037.pdf)). This is a historical study-period benchmark, not a 2025 market-share estimate. Meanwhile, the PBOC's 2024 taxonomy makes clear that non-bank payments include bank-account-funded transactions; platform interface share therefore does not equal deposit or clearing share ([PBOC 2024 payment-system report, footnotes pp. 5–8](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/en/3688247/3688978/3709143/2025080817520765198/2025040114593718714.pdf)).

**Credit underwriting.** Cross-country BIS work characterises fintech credit as digital origination without a traditional branch network, often using proprietary e-commerce and payment data; China was the world's largest fintech-credit market in that study period ([BIS, *Fintech credit markets around the world*, 2018](https://www.bis.org/publications/fintech-credit-markets-around-world-size-drivers-and-policy-issues)). Chinese microdata research found a big-tech lender was more likely than banks to establish new SME lending relationships during monetary easing, consistent with information, monitoring and risk-management advantages from platform data ([BIS Working Paper 1084, 2023](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1084-big-tech-credit-and-monetary-policy-transmission-micro-level-evidence-china)).

**Deposit and wealth competition.** BIS analysis found that deposit-like big-tech money-market funds affected competing Chinese banks' deposit growth and increased deposit-rate pass-through, showing that platform wealth products can pressure bank funding even when underlying assets remain inside the regulated financial system ([BCBS Working Paper 43, 2024, China case study](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/wp43.pdf)). Platform business models can lower financial-service cost and increase inclusion, but concentration can create monopoly risks; incumbents can also adopt platform models ([BIS Working Paper 986, 2021](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-986-platform-based-business-models-and-financial-inclusion)).

**Fees and revenue mix.** In 2010, the banking industry's revenue comprised 66% net interest income, 21% net fees/commissions, 12% investment income and 1% other income ([CBRC 2010 Annual Report, p. 32](https://www.nfra.gov.cn/chinese/subject/subject/nianbao2010/english/1.pdf)). By 2025, ICBC's net fee and commission income was 13.87% of operating income and ABC's was 12.14%; CMB, whose wealth and retail franchise is stronger, reported non-interest income at 36.08% of operating income ([ICBC 2025 Annual Report](https://v.icbc.com.cn/userfiles/Resources/ICBCLTD/download/2026/2025AnnualReport.pdf); [ABC 2025 shareholder-meeting filing](https://vip.stock.finance.sina.com.cn/corp/view/vCB_AllBulletinDetail.php?id=12066293&stockid=601288); [CMB 2025 annual-report transcript](https://financialfilings.com/filings/china-merchants-bank-co-ltd/annual-report/2026/60237223/)). These are not directly comparable categories—“non-interest income” is broader than net fees—and they do not isolate fintech causality.

**Regulated coexistence.** China's non-bank payment rules encourage payment institutions and commercial banks to cooperate, while payment-account balances are not bank deposits and generally may not earn interest for customers ([PBOC, implementation rules for supervision of non-bank payment institutions, 2024](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/tiaofasi/144941/144953/5174993/index.html)). The PBOC has also described mobile payment as substituting for cash and bank-card use while calling for compatible coexistence and collaboration among payment methods ([PBOC payment-policy statement](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/rmyh/3963412/3963426/2025092319241131243/index.html)).

**Banks' competitive response.** ICBC reported 18.94m corporate digital customers and 8.36m corporate digital MAU in 2025; CMB reported 129m consumer MAU, and its retail franchise generated more than half of revenue and profit ([ICBC 2025 Annual Report](https://v.icbc.com.cn/userfiles/Resources/ICBCLTD/download/2026/2025AnnualReport.pdf); [CMB 2025 annual-report transcript](https://financialfilings.com/filings/china-merchants-bank-co-ltd/annual-report/2026/60237223/)). ABC combined branch reach with digital inclusion: its rural mobile-banking MAU exceeded 52m in 2025 ([ABC 2025 Annual Report transcript](https://financialfilings.com/filings/agricultural-bank-of-china-limited/annual-report/2026/60236936/)).

### Inferences

- Fintech lowered customer-acquisition friction and reset UX expectations, forcing banks to compete on app reliability, instant servicing, personalisation and ecosystem breadth. The bank MAU and off-counter evidence shows the response, while the BIS evidence identifies the platform-data advantage.
- Merchant/payment transaction fees and simple distribution margins became less defensible; advice, custody, differentiated product manufacture and integrated banking relationships became more important. CMB's high non-interest share and bank-WMP growth show that banks adapted rather than abandoning wealth.
- Fintech and banks “grow each other” through a feedback loop: platforms generate transaction/behaviour data and expand digital usage; bank accounts fund wallets, banks provide safeguarding/clearing and regulated credit capacity; bank APIs and digital products then deepen platform commerce. Regulation constrains pure disintermediation by requiring payment institutions to remain connected to the banking system.
- Underwriting competition is strongest in small, short-duration, data-rich credit. Banks retain an advantage in mortgages, corporate/project lending and policy-aligned credit where funding tenor, collateral, covenants, capital and relationship knowledge matter more than clickstream data.

### Gaps

- A causal estimate of fintech's effect on bank fee income needs bank-level panel regressions and product-level fee disclosure. The available trend evidence is insufficient to attribute fee changes to fintech rather than regulation, market volatility, rate cuts or fee-remission policies.
- Platform credit must be split into on-balance-sheet loans, co-lending, loan facilitation and securitisation. Gross merchandise/loan origination volume is not comparable with bank loan balances.
- Platform wealth “AUM” may refer to distributed fund balances, advised assets or assets in affiliated managers; compare only like-for-like product stocks.

## Where do banks retain durable advantages, and where have they lost the interface?

### Takeaway

Banks retain the deposit franchise, regulatory licence, low-cost funding, capital, long-duration asset/liability management, large-ticket underwriting, payments settlement, custody, cross-border networks and policy execution. Fintech firms captured much of the daily discovery and merchant-payment interface and established the UX benchmark for small-ticket payments, embedded finance and self-directed investment. The equilibrium is layered: fintech often owns the first click; banks usually own or support the regulated account, funding, settlement, custody and risk-bearing underneath it.

### Inferences

- Banks have not lost “payments” as infrastructure; they lost part of payments as customer-visible initiation. The PBOC definitions explicitly show that non-bank payment activity can draw on bank accounts.
- The most durable bank moat is the combination of trusted deposit funding, regulation/capital and multi-product relationship data. Any one element can be attacked; the integrated balance sheet is much harder to replicate.
- Large banks' asset share rising to 44.5% by 2026 Q2, while third-party payment frequency soared, is the clearest coexistence result: platform engagement and bank balance-sheet concentration increased at the same time ([NFRA 2026 Q2](https://www.nfra.gov.cn/en/view/pages/ItemDetail.html?docId=1270720); [PBOC 2024](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/en/3688247/3688978/3709143/2025080817520765198/2025040114593718714.pdf)).
- The investment question is no longer “banks or fintech?” but who controls each profit pool and who has bargaining power at each layer. For banks, watch deposit beta, NIM, app MAU, fee-income mix, retail AUM, credit cost and API/platform partnerships. For fintech, watch payment frequency, monetisation per user, funded-vs-facilitated credit mix, funding partners, take rate and regulatory capital intensity.

### Gaps

- No defensible single statistic can split all “transactions” into a bank share and fintech share. The analysis must present at least payment count, payment value, funding source and initiation channel separately.
- Cross-platform bank-account linking, merchant acquisition, QR acceptance and clearing-network data would improve the layer-by-layer market map.
- Comparable unit economics—customer acquisition cost, cost to serve, take rate, credit loss and capital consumed—are rarely disclosed on the same basis.
