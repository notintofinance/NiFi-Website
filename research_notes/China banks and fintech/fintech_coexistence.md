# China fintech/platform growth, client usage and coexistence with banks, 2010–2026

## 1. How did client usage evolve across transactions, wealth, investment, loans and business services?

### Takeaway
China's fintech story moved through four distinct stages: payment licensing and e-commerce checkout (2010–2012); wallet-led mass adoption and the first “super-app” finance products (2013–2016); rapid cross-selling into wealth, credit and insurance (2017–2020); and regulated coexistence with licensed banks and asset managers (2021–2026). By 2026, Alipay and Weixin/WeChat remained mass-market customer interfaces, but the fastest recent growth was in regulated digital banks and licensed distribution/brokerage ecosystems; non-bank consumer-credit facilitators were contracting in China after the October 2025 loan-assistance rules and a mid-2026 funding shock.

### Cited Findings

#### Milestone chronology

- **2010–2012 — a licensed payment layer formed.** The PBOC's 2010 Administrative Measures for Payment Services of Non-Financial Institutions created the licensing perimeter subsequently cited in payment-industry rules; it converted Alipay, Tenpay and peers from lightly regulated checkout tools into licensed payment institutions. The same period also saw Alibaba's finance arm begin providing short-term funds to merchants on its platforms in 2010. Tiantian Fund was among China's first four independent fund-sales licensees in 2012. — [PBOC reference to Order No. 2 of 2010](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/english/130733/2025080815071811497/index.html); [BIS on Alibaba/Ant SME credit since 2010](https://www.bis.org/publications/fintech-credit-markets-around-world-size-drivers-and-policy-issues); [Tiantian Fund licensing history](https://www.jiemian.com/article/902037.html)
- **2013–2016 — payments became the acquisition funnel for wealth and credit.** Third-party mobile-payment value expanded dramatically: a peer-reviewed study reports total mobile-payment value increasing from RMB14.5 trillion in 2013 to RMB277.4 trillion in 2018, an 80% annual growth rate; over the same period Alipay active users rose from just over 100 million to 900 million and WeChat Pay users from roughly 350 million to 1.1 billion. — [Asian Economic Papers/MIT Press](https://direct.mit.edu/asep/article/19/3/1/93345/Mobile-Payment-in-China-Practice-and-Its-Effects)
- Alipay's payment dominance briefly peaked as smartphones and QR codes took off: iResearch estimated 82.3% of third-party mobile-payment GMV for Alipay and 10.6% for Tenpay in 2014; the market itself reached RMB5.99 trillion that year, up 391%. The estimate excludes banks and UnionPay and therefore is a **share within third-party mobile payment**, not a share of all transactions. — [SWIFT paper reproducing iResearch's 2014 share chart](https://www.swift.com/sites/default/files/files/siwp-no-2015-001-aml-risks-of-the-third-party-payment-providers_final.pdf); [Mobile World Live/iResearch market-size summary](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/asia-pacific/chinas-mobile-payment-gmv-quadruples/)
- Yu'e Bao, launched inside Alipay in 2013, showed how a payment balance could become an investment account with almost no additional acquisition cost. Within five years it had more than RMB1 trillion of assets and about 350 million customers, becoming the world's largest money-market fund. The fund assets belonged to the fund manager/investors, not Ant's balance sheet. — [BIS Annual Economic Report 2019](https://www.bis.org/publications/aer-2019/big-tech-finance-opportunities-risks)
- Tencent's 2014 Lunar New Year red-envelope campaign turned social messaging into peer-to-peer payment; the service then moved into offline merchant QR payment. WeBank, opened in 2014 and launched its Weilidai unsecured loan in 2015, had more than 6 million individual customers in its first reported year. — [WeBank 2015 annual-report coverage](https://www.cs.com.cn/tzjj/jjdt/201603/t20160330_4936298.html); [WeBank company history/product](https://webank.com/characteristic/financial)
- **2017–2020 — super-app finance scaled.** By Q4 2016, iResearch put Alipay at 55% and Tenpay at 37% of third-party mobile payments, a durable two-player structure. In 2019, non-bank mobile-payment transactions were estimated at RMB204.9 trillion; in Q2 2020 Analysys estimated Alipay at 55.39% and Tenpay at 38.47%. — [iResearch 2016 share chart](https://e-research.siam.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/IMBA-2018-IS-The-Analysis-of-Critical-Factors-for-Mobile-Payment-Competition-in-China-Evidence-from-Alipay_compressed.pdf); [Caixin/Analysys 2019–2020 figures](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2021-02-08/four-things-to-know-about-threats-to-chinas-payment-giants-101662049.html)
- Tencent reported more than 1 billion daily payment transactions in 2018, with commercial payments already more than half of transactions and tens of millions of merchants connected; since Q4 2019 it has handled over 1 billion average daily **commercial** payment transactions. These are counts, not TPV, and cannot be compared directly with Ant's value-based TPV. — [Tencent 2018 annual report](https://financialfilings.com/filings/tencent-holdings-limited/annual-report/2019/19505208/); [Tencent 2022 strategy presentation](https://static.www.tencent.com/uploads/2022/11/29/cea3aa36692ded270993b2bd15110521.pdf)
- Ant's June 2020 scale captures the mature super-app model: more than 1 billion Alipay annual active users, 711 million monthly active users, 729 million annual digital-finance users, over 80 million monthly active merchants and more than 2,000 partner financial institutions. Twelve-month mainland TPV was RMB118 trillion; enabled balances were RMB1.732 trillion consumer credit and RMB422 billion SME credit; enabled investment AUM was RMB4.099 trillion; insurance premiums/contributions were RMB52 billion. — [Ant draft prospectus, pp. 167, 189–191](https://wynthia.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/ant-draft-ipo-prospectus-2020-08-read.pdf); [HKEX document summarizing the prospectus](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2020/1029/2020102901335.pdf)
- Ant's payments did not merely coexist with its financial products: its prospectus says digital-finance activity by Alipay users retained since 2015 increased more than tenfold through the twelve months to June 2020. The wallet was the high-frequency funnel; credit, investment and insurance generated substantially more monetization per engaged user. — [Ant prospectus](https://wynthia.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/ant-draft-ipo-prospectus-2020-08-read.pdf)
- JD Digits followed the same commerce-to-finance pattern around JD Baitiao and Jintiao but disclosed more enterprise technology revenue. Revenue rose from RMB9.07 billion in 2017 to RMB18.20 billion in 2019 (41.7% CAGR), while financial-institution digital solutions rose from 17.1% to 34.2% of revenue; JD Baitiao annual active users rose from 24.9 million in 2017 to 57.8 million in 2019. — [JD Digits STAR Market prospectus](https://static.sse.com.cn/stock/disclosure/announcement/c/202009/000712_20200911_P5EX.pdf); [prospectus coverage with Baitiao users](https://finance.sina.cn/2020-09-12/detail-iivhvpwy6370970.d.html)
- WeBank demonstrates the scale of a fully licensed, branchless bank inside a fintech ecosystem. It grew from more than 6 million individual customers in 2015 to 270 million consumers and 1.88 million SMEs at end-2020; its borrowers were disproportionately underserved (80% of Weilidai users lacked a university degree, and about 100,000 new SME customers in 2020 had never received bank credit). — [2015 annual-report coverage](https://www.cs.com.cn/tzjj/jjdt/201603/t20160330_4936298.html); [BIS paper on virtual banking/WeBank, Box A](https://www.bis.org/publ/bppdf/bispap120.pdf)
- MYbank's cumulative small-business reach grew from 12.27 million in 2018 to 53 million in 2023 and more than 68 million in 2024, evolving from pure microcredit into payments, cash management and wealth distribution. — [Xinhua on MYbank 2018](https://kr.xinhuanet.com/2019-05/07/c_138039597.htm); [MYbank 2023 annual-report release](https://via.ritzau.dk/pressemeddelelse/13823278/mybank?lang=en&publisherId=90456); [China Securities Journal on MYbank 2024](https://www.cs.com.cn/ssgs/gsxl/202504/t20250430_6489293.html)
- Independent investment platforms scaled as distinct client interfaces. Tiantian Fund sales rose from RMB229.7 billion in 2014 to RMB743.3 billion in 2015, RMB1.298 trillion in 2020 and RMB2.239 trillion in 2021. Its 2020 platform averaged 2.36 million daily active visitors and 200.1 million subscription/purchase transactions. — [2014 annual-report coverage](https://www.chnfund.com/article/AR2015021609132542477881); [2015 annual-report coverage](https://www.jiemian.com/article/902037.html); [East Money 2020 annual report](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2021-04-27/1209805869.PDF); [East Money 2021 annual-report coverage](https://finance.sina.cn/fund/jjyj/2022-03-18/detail-imcwipih9272776.d.html)
- Futu's investing interface grew from 198,382 funded/pay clients and HK$87.1 billion client assets at end-2019 to 516,721 and HK$285.2 billion in 2020, then 1.244 million and HK$407.8 billion in 2021. This growth is **global/Hong Kong-heavy**, not a valid measure of mainland securities market share. — [Futu 2020 results](https://ir.futuholdings.com/news-releases/news-release-details/futu-announces-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2020-unaudited); [Futu 2021 Form 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1754581/000110465922035332/futu-20211231x20f.htm)
- Lufax's 2020 platform served different needs: 46.2 million registered wealth users, 14.9 million active investors and RMB426.6 billion client assets, alongside 14.48 million cumulative borrowers and RMB545.1 billion retail-credit balance, funded through 63 institutional partners (51 banks). It was a mass-affluent wealth shelf plus an O2O credit distributor, not a payments wallet. — [Ping An 2020 annual report, Lufax section](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2021/0223/2021022300744.pdf); [Lufax FY2020 results](https://ir-tc.lufaxholding.com/News-Releases?item=21)
- **2021–2023 — usage persisted, but products became more visibly regulated and separated.** Ant's mandated rectification required payment to return to small-value convenience, a financial holding company application, licensed personal credit reporting, stronger data protection and clearer boundaries between payment and Huabei/Jiebei. Huabei/Jiebei interfaces subsequently labelled bank-funded credit separately from Ant Consumer Finance-funded products. — [Ant rectification statement filed with SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465921049222/a21-12722_1ex99d1.htm); [Ant product restructuring summary](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/24/ant-restructure-jiebei-huabei/)
- The P2P channel disappeared rather than growing alongside banks: P2P originations reached nearly 40% of new bank loans in June 2016, fell below 10% by June 2018, and all operating platforms were closed by mid-November 2020. Licensed bank-funded platform credit replaced retail-investor-funded P2P. — [BIS P2P analysis](https://www.bis.org/publications/p2p-lending-china); [PBOC/CBIRC chairman speech](https://www.bis.org/speeches/20201222-fintech-developments-challenges-supervision-china)
- **2024–2026 — mature payments, expanding regulated wealth, and a lending reset.** In 2024 banks processed 210.98 billion mobile-payment transactions worth RMB563.70 trillion, while non-bank payment institutions processed 1.34 trillion online-payment transactions worth RMB331.68 trillion. These series overlap economically because a wallet payment can be funded from a bank account and cleared through NetsUnion/UnionPay; they must not be added to infer a conventional-bank/fintech split. — [PBOC Payment System Report 2024, p. 4](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/en/3688247/3688978/3709143/2025080817520765198/2025040114593718714.pdf)
- An AMRO 2025 regional financial-stability report cites 2024 mobile-payment shares of 55% for Alipay and 39% for WeChat Pay, consistent with the 2020 duopoly. Treat these as market-research estimates rather than regulatory market-share statistics. — [AMRO Annual Consultation Report 2025, Box 3.2](https://amro-asia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/AMRO-AFSR-2025-Full-Report-Web.pdf)
- By Q1 2026 Weixin/WeChat had 1.432 billion combined MAU; this is an app-account measure, not payment users or mainland unique individuals. Ant's 2025 sustainability release continued to describe Alipay as connecting more than one billion users to more than 10,000 types of partner services, but did not refresh TPV or active-finance users. — [Tencent Q1 2026 filing](https://static.www.tencent.com/uploads/2026/06/09/33b0db632d62324c7c2f902989ccb0c6.pdf); [Ant 2025 Sustainability Report release](https://www.antgroup.com/en/news-media/press-releases/1781661600000)
- WeBank had 424 million valid individual customers at end-2024, RMB436 billion loans, RMB3.244 trillion of reported assets under management, RMB38.13 billion revenue and RMB10.90 billion net profit. By April 2026 it reported more than 440 million personal customers and more than 7.6 million MSMEs. — [WeBank 2024 annual report](https://tctp.tctpwebankcdn.net/owb-admin/data/appsystems/owb-admin/1756804158938-13423.pdf); [WeBank operating-scale page, data to April 2026](https://webank.com/characteristic/financial)
- MYbank ended 2024 with RMB471.0 billion assets, RMB298.5 billion loans, RMB344.1 billion deposits, RMB21.31 billion revenue, RMB3.17 billion profit, more than 68 million cumulative small-business clients and more than RMB1 trillion client AUM. At end-2025 its broader financial services had reached 77.22 million small-business operators and AUM exceeded RMB1.2 trillion. — [MYbank 2024 annual report](https://qxb-pdf-osscache.qixin.com/AnBaseinfo/7f4fb677abdf6a905d911992520c8b93.pdf); [Xinhua on MYbank 2025 ESG report](https://www1.xinhuanet.com/tech/20260514/59c0dbabb44c410997fcf1dad748f9f3/c.html)
- East Money/Tiantian Fund recovered with capital markets: in 2025 East Money's total revenue was RMB16.07 billion (+38.5%), securities-service revenue RMB12.54 billion (+47.6%) and financial e-commerce/fund-distribution revenue RMB3.18 billion (+12.0%). Its brokerage generated RMB38.46 trillion of stock/fund turnover; Tiantian generated RMB2.606 trillion fund sales, held RMB770.1 billion non-money-market mutual funds and RMB445.6 billion equity funds. — [East Money 2025 annual report](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-03-20/1225019509.PDF); [annual-report figures summarized by East Money](https://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202603193677729731.html)
- Futu ended 2025 with 29.2 million global users, 5.95 million brokerage accounts, 3.37 million funded accounts and HK$1.233 trillion client assets; by Q2 2026 client assets were HK$1.40 trillion, quarterly trading volume HK$6.42 trillion and wealth-management client assets HK$180.2 billion. Again, this is a global licensed-brokerage ecosystem and should not be classified as mainland domestic broker market share. — [Futu FY2025 results](https://ir.futuholdings.com/news-releases/news-release-details/futu-announces-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-unaudited); [Futu Q2 2026 results](https://ir.futuholdings.com/news-releases/news-release-details/futu-announces-second-quarter-2026-unaudited-financial-results/)
- The listed online lenders show a 2026 China contraction despite large registered bases. FinVolution's mainland Q2 2026 volume fell 19.3% YoY to RMB41.0 billion and outstanding fell 13.3% to RMB65.4 billion, although cumulative registered users reached 192.8 million. Qfin's Q2 loan volume fell 25.1% to RMB63.4 billion and outstanding fell 23.2% to RMB107.6 billion. Lexin's Q2 originations rose 4.8% to RMB55.4 billion, but outstanding fell 11.4% to RMB93.7 billion and net income fell to RMB101 million from RMB511 million. — [FinVolution Q2 2026](https://ir.finvgroup.com/news-releases/news-release-details/finvolution-group-reports-second-quarter-2026-unaudited); [Qfin Q2 2026](https://360finance.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/qfin-holdings-announces-second-quarter-and-interim-2026); [Lexin Q2 2026](https://lexinfintech.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lexinfintech-holdings-ltd-reports-second-quarter-2026-unaudited)

### Inferences

- **The dominant client journey is “transaction first, finance second.”** Payment generated identity, merchant and behavioural data plus daily engagement; Yu'e Bao, Huabei/Jiebei, Licaitong, Weilidai and insurance were then embedded at the moment of need. Ant's 729 million finance users versus 711 million monthly Alipay users in 2020 shows how far cross-selling had progressed, though the denominators differ. — [Ant prospectus](https://wynthia.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/ant-draft-ipo-prospectus-2020-08-read.pdf)
- **Different ecosystems attract different jobs-to-be-done.** Alipay is commerce/payment-led; WeChat Pay is social and daily-life-led; MYbank is merchant/SME-led; WeBank is mass-retail and small-business lending/wealth; East Money is market-information-to-fund/brokerage conversion; Futu is trading/community and international brokerage; Lufax is small-business credit and mass-affluent wealth; Qfin, FinVolution and Lexin are primarily unsecured consumer-credit acquisition/risk engines. — [Ant prospectus](https://wynthia.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/ant-draft-ipo-prospectus-2020-08-read.pdf); [Tencent investor presentation](https://static.www.tencent.com/uploads/2022/11/29/cea3aa36692ded270993b2bd15110521.pdf); [MYbank 2024 report](https://qxb-pdf-osscache.qixin.com/AnBaseinfo/7f4fb677abdf6a905d911992520c8b93.pdf); [East Money 2025 report](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-03-20/1225019509.PDF); [Lufax Q2 2026](https://ir.lufaxholding.com/2026-08-18-Lufax-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results)
- **Registered-user counts exaggerate current monetizable use.** Lexin had 245 million registered users but only 8.2 million annual active users in 2025; Qfin and FinVolution similarly publish cumulative registrations/borrowers alongside much smaller quarterly active borrower bases. Investment work should use MAU, transacting users, funded accounts or current borrowers rather than registrations. — [Lexin FY2025 results](https://ir.lexinfintech.com/static-files/64d8c706-dc02-415d-9624-f7a4d574b992); [FinVolution FY2025 results](https://ir.finvgroup.com/news-releases/news-release-details/finvolution-group-reports-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-year-2025); [Qfin FY2025 results](https://ir.qifu.tech/news-releases/news-release-details/qfin-holdings-announces-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025)

### Gaps

- Ant has not published a prospectus-quality domestic TPV, active digital-finance user, CreditTech balance or segment-revenue update after 2020. Current Alipay “more than one billion users” is not defined as MAU, annual active or transacting users. Bloomberg/private-company data may help, but any estimate must be labelled.
- Tencent does not disclose Weixin Pay transacting users, TPV, payment take rate, Licaitong AUM or stand-alone fintech revenue; its reported “FinTech and Business Services” segment includes cloud and other enterprise services.
- JD Digits/JD Technology withdrew its listing process and has not maintained a comparable public operating-KPI series after the 2020 prospectus.

## 2. Which fintechs own the customer interface, and which fund, underwrite or hold the risk?

### Takeaway
“Fintech” is not one balance-sheet model. Ant/Tencent and independent loan platforms generally own discovery, data, decisioning and servicing while regulated institutions supply products and most funding; WeBank and MYbank are themselves licensed banks; East Money and Futu own regulated broker/distributor entities; and Lufax moved in the opposite direction from capital-light facilitation toward bearing most credit risk. Regulation since 2020 has pushed the whole sector toward visible licensed product providers, bank-controlled underwriting and explicit risk/capital responsibility.

### Inferences

- **Interface ownership and balance-sheet ownership should be measured separately.** Ant could originate or enable RMB2.154 trillion of credit while funding/underwriting only about 2% directly; the platform owned customer attention and earned a revenue share, while banks/ABS investors supplied funding. Counting all of this as “fintech loans” and again as bank loans double-counts the same assets. — [Ant prospectus](https://wynthia.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/ant-draft-ipo-prospectus-2020-08-read.pdf)
- **The profitable equilibrium is hybrid, not replacement.** Platforms lower acquisition/servicing costs and reach thin-file consumers or micro merchants; banks contribute cheap deposits, legal lending capacity and capital. Regulation is moving approval, pricing and disclosure power back to the bank without eliminating the platform front end. — [2021 commercial-bank internet-loan notice](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zcfb/zgdwjjmywg/art/2021/art_b3faa0277ac64fefa328864628d4292c.html); [2025 loan-assistance notice](https://app.www.gov.cn/govdata/gov/202504/04/526105/article.html)
- **Risk-bearing is becoming the key valuation discriminator.** Qfin and FinVolution have roughly half their outstanding in capital-light/technology modes, while Lufax bore 93.2% by Q2 2026. The former are more fee/platform-like but still exposed to funding partners and regulatory pricing; the latter is economically closer to a specialty bank/consumer-finance company and absorbs credit-cost volatility. — [Qfin Q2 2026](https://360finance.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/qfin-holdings-announces-second-quarter-and-interim-2026); [FinVolution Q2 2026](https://ir.finvgroup.com/news-releases/news-release-details/finvolution-group-reports-second-quarter-2026-unaudited); [Lufax Q2 2026](https://ir.lufaxholding.com/2026-08-18-Lufax-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results)

### Gaps

- Current Ant Consumer Finance loan balance, Ant's direct/co-funded capital contribution, Huabei/Jiebei active borrowers and credit losses are not publicly disclosed at the standard of the 2020 prospectus.
- WeBank does not publicly split RMB436 billion of 2024 loans between retained, syndicated/co-lent and pure technology/referral exposure in a readily comparable KPI.
- JD Technology's post-2020 split between Baitiao/Jintiao credit risk, institutional funding and pure technology revenue is unavailable publicly.

## 3. What market shares can be responsibly estimated?

### Takeaway
The only reasonably defensible share series is **within third-party mobile payments**, where Alipay and Tenpay/WeChat Pay jointly held roughly 90–96% from 2016 through 2024. Wealth distribution can be ranked by institution and holdings, with Ant Fund first and bank/fintech channels intermingled. There is no clean published “bank versus fintech” share for transaction value, consumer lending or SME lending because banks fund/settle much of the fintech volume and licensed digital banks blur the categories.

- **Payments market share answers “which app owns the last-mile interface,” not “who moves all the money.”** The wallet can initiate the transaction, a bank account/card can fund it, NetsUnion/UnionPay can clear it and a bank can settle it. - **Wealth distribution is the clearest direct channel substitution.** Ant Fund and Tiantian take shelf space and client attention from bank branches while handing assets to regulated fund managers/custodians; banks remain both rivals (CMB/ICBC/CCB distribution) and upstream product/custody providers. — [AMAC portal](https://www.amac.org.cn/sjtj/datastatistics/fundsalesindustrydata/); [Ant Fortune product model](https://www.antgroup.com/en/business-development/digital-finance-tab-details/ant-wealth)
- **Credit “share” must be presented as three stacked measures:** (1) platform-enabled/originated balance or volume, (2) platform risk-bearing balance, and (3) licensed lender funding/on-balance-sheet balance. - No PBOC table reconciles bank-mobile and non-bank-wallet transactions at the underlying economic-transaction level. A conventional-bank/fintech transaction-share estimate would require proprietary de-duplicated acquiring data.
- No regulator publishes a complete cross-platform online consumer/SME-credit table split by originator, funder, guarantor and ultimate risk holder.
- AMAC's distribution holdings are available by institution, but a long 2010–2026 channel-share series (banks, brokers, independent third parties, direct fund sales) would require downloading archived quarterly tables and standardizing changing definitions.

## 4. How did regulation reshape growth and bank partnerships?

### Takeaway
Regulation evolved from “license and allow experimentation” to “same activity, same risk, same regulation.” The critical economic changes were central clearing and zero-interest reserve of payment float; elimination of P2P; bank-controlled underwriting and minimum partner capital in co-lending; licensed financial-holding/consumer-finance/credit-reporting structures; data and antitrust obligations; and, from 2025–2026, hard constraints on total borrower cost, platform fees, whitelists and online marketing. These rules did not eliminate fintech; they forced it into transparent partnerships with licensed institutions.

### Inferences

- The 2020–2023 correction was a **business-model repricing**, not simply an anti-tech campaign: Ant's value proposition shifted from thinly capitalized balance-sheet substitution toward regulated customer acquisition, technology and distribution. — [Ant prospectus risk/funding model](https://wynthia.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/ant-draft-ipo-prospectus-2020-08-read.pdf); [rectification plan](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465921049222/a21-12722_1ex99d1.htm)
- The 2025–2026 rules increase banks' bargaining power: banks choose whitelisted platforms, control pricing/approval and can terminate partners whose fees or data are inadequate. Platforms retain reach and models but lose opaque fee extraction. — [2025 NFRA notice](https://app.www.gov.cn/govdata/gov/202504/04/526105/article.html); [2026 NFRA marketing rules](https://www.nfra.gov.cn/en/view/pages/ItemDetail.html?docId=1257542&itemId=981)
- The mid-2026 funding shock is consistent with this transmission channel: Qfin reported a sudden industry-wide liquidity shock in late June; FinVolution expected material Q3 China contraction due to tighter institutional funding; Lexin expected a significant sequential contraction and possible Q3 loss. — [Qfin Q2 2026](https://360finance.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/qfin-holdings-announces-second-quarter-and-interim-2026); [FinVolution Q2 2026](https://ir.finvgroup.com/news-releases/news-release-details/finvolution-group-reports-second-quarter-2026-unaudited); [Lexin Q2 2026](https://lexinfintech.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lexinfintech-holdings-ltd-reports-second-quarter-2026-unaudited)

- Ant's financial-holding-company application outcome and consolidated regulatory capital are not transparently reported in current public disclosures.
- Company-by-company impact of the October 2025 loan-assistance notice on funding-partner counts, APRs, fee caps and approval rates will require 2026 lender/bank disclosures and conference-call transcripts.

## 5. How do banks and fintechs complement, compete with and grow one another, and what Bloomberg data would close the gaps?

### Takeaway
Fintechs won the interface and high-frequency use cases; banks retained deposits, settlement rails, licences and most risk capital. Their relationship is therefore simultaneous competition and vertical partnership: fintech compresses branch/payment/fund-distribution economics and forces better mobile UX, while banks fund, custody, clear and manufacture the products that platforms distribute. The equilibrium has shifted from unchecked platform leverage to regulated embedded finance.

### Cited Findings

- **Payments complementarity:** wallets supply QR acceptance, merchant software and user engagement; bank accounts/cards usually supply funds, and NetsUnion/UnionPay plus banks clear/settle. The central-clearing regime explicitly replaced opaque direct links between each platform and many banks. — [BIS 2019 payment architecture](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2019e.pdf)
- **Credit complementarity:** Ant partnered with about 100 banks at June 2020 and banks/ABS underwrote 98% of its enabled credit; Lufax had 51 bank funding partners in 2020; Qfin had 162 financial-institution partners by end-2024. Platforms expand borrower reach and automate risk/servicing, while banks monetize deposits and capital. — [Ant prospectus](https://wynthia.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/ant-draft-ipo-prospectus-2020-08-read.pdf); [Ping An/Lufax 2020](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2021/0223/2021022300744.pdf); [Qfin FY2024](https://ir.qifu.tech/news-releases/news-release-details/qifu-technology-announces-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024)
- **Wealth complementarity and competition:** Ant Fortune works with more than 150 asset managers and offers their funds/products to hundreds of millions of users; MYbank distributes third-party products from 23 bank wealth-management subsidiaries. Yet Ant Fund and Tiantian compete directly with CMB/ICBC/CCB for the distribution relationship and trail fee. — [Ant Fortune](https://www.antgroup.com/en/business-development/digital-finance-tab-details/ant-wealth); [MYbank 2024](https://www.cs.com.cn/ssgs/gsxl/202504/t20250430_6489293.html); [AMAC rankings](https://www.amac.org.cn/sjtj/datastatistics/fundsalesindustrydata/)

### Inferences

- The investable winners are likely to be institutions that control a high-frequency interface **and** have a defensible licensed/risk partnership: Tencent/Ant in payments and distribution, East Money in information-to-brokerage conversion, and WeBank/MYbank in low-cost digital banking. Pure traffic/referral lenders face the highest rule/funding elasticity. — [Tencent 2025 annual report](https://financialfilings.com/filings/tencent-holdings-limited/annual-report/2026/33896269/); [East Money 2025 annual report](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-03-20/1225019509.PDF); [2025 loan-assistance rules](https://app.www.gov.cn/govdata/gov/202504/04/526105/article.html)
- Banks were not “displaced” so much as pushed down-stack: from branch/interface owners to product manufacturers, custodians, funders and settlement institutions. Fintech captured fee pools and data at the front end; regulation has since returned underwriting, suitability, cost disclosure and capital accountability to licensed entities. — [BIS payment architecture](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2019e.pdf); [2026 online-marketing rules](https://www.nfra.gov.cn/en/view/pages/ItemDetail.html?docId=1257542&itemId=981)
- For valuation, do not compare fintech TPV/origination to bank assets. Compare fintech **net revenue/take rate and risk-bearing balance** with bank **net interest/fee income, loans and capital**; separately compare interface metrics such as transacting users, TPV, fund holdings and originations. — [Ant funding model](https://wynthia.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/ant-draft-ipo-prospectus-2020-08-read.pdf); [Qfin metric definitions](https://360finance.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/qfin-holdings-announces-second-quarter-and-interim-2026)

### Gaps

- Bloomberg consensus cannot solve private-company disclosure gaps unless analysts publish sourced estimates; Ant and private digital-bank estimates should be triangulated against shareholder filings and regulator/company annual reports.
- A complete 2010–2026 financial panel requires manual historical KPI extraction because company definitions changed (registered vs active; facilitated vs originated; outstanding excluding different delinquency windows; AUM vs client assets; global vs mainland).
- Payment-market share after 2020 remains dependent on research-house estimates. The PBOC publishes system volumes, not Alipay/Tenpay brand shares.
