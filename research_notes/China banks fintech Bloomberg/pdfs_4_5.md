### Inferences

- These reports are useful as time-stamped industry expectations and historical private-company evidence. They should be integrated as **BI research estimates**, not as Bloomberg standardized fields or current terminal consensus.
- The combination is especially valuable for showing how the pre-crackdown 2019 bull case was repriced into a 2021 “more curbs” case. That is a regulatory-regime comparison, not a continuous 2019-2026 forecast series.

### Gaps

- The PDFs do not expose BI ticker/function identifiers, chart download mnemonics, or machine-readable field definitions.
- They provide no 2021-26 operating history, no 2026 forecasts, and no listed-company valuation panel.
- Because `4.pdf` is a localization product, any wording dispute ultimately requires the English original; it is not attached.

### Inferences

- `5.pdf` gives a credible historical answer to the user’s question about client usage: online banks were being used not just for payments, but also for deposits, loans, money-market funds, wealth/fund products and co-lending. However, the survey measures which products banks offered, not how many clients used each product.
- The funding-mix transition is strategically important. WeBank’s shift from an 894% LDR in 2017 to 78% in 2018 illustrates how ecosystem distribution can be converted into a deposit franchise; that is why WeBank belongs in the report’s licensed-digital-bank category rather than the non-bank facilitator category.
- The 2019 fee forecast shows the intended hybrid economics: payment/ecosystem traffic acquires users; deposits fund the balance sheet; co-lending and wealth distribution generate fee income. This directly supports the main report’s “regulated co-opetition” thesis.
- The 2021 scenario is a useful ex ante measure of regulatory sensitivity: the largest proportional downside was expected in credit (35% below the limited-curbs case), while payments and wealth were expected to remain large but grow more slowly.

- No company-level transaction counts or TPV are supplied for Ant, Tencent, WeBank, MYbank, JD, Qfin, FinVolution, Lexin or Lufax.

### Inferences

- **Recommended exact addition 1 — historical digital-bank table.** Add a compact 2015-18 WeBank/MYbank table containing loan balance, deposit/interbank/other-liability mix, LDR, net fees and fee share of revenue. Label it “company filings compiled by BI; historical actual/estimate as presented” and keep 2025E in a separate shaded/forecast column.
- **Recommended exact addition 2 — expectation-reset box.** Add a short box contrasting the 2019 bull/bear online-bank forecasts with the 2021 more-curbs/limited-curbs fintech-market scenarios. This makes the regulatory turning point measurable without confusing either scenario with 2025 actuals.
- **Recommended exact addition 3 — client use cases.** In the user-perspective section, add the 135-direct-bank product-availability evidence: money-market funds (90), loans (83), wealth products (80), deposits/funds (60 each), payments (47), life services (31), precious metals (20), insurance (11), credit cards (7). Explicitly say it measures offerings, not customer usage.
- **Recommended exact addition 4 — funding interdependence.** Add the 2021 funding-partner counts as a qualitative network-breadth indicator, with a bold caveat that number of partners is not outstanding funding. This supports the finding that fintech scales through regulated financial institutions.
- **Recommended exact addition 5 — share-source range.** Preserve the existing roughly 94% combined wallet-share conclusion and footnote the BI/iResearch 2Q20 split of 55.6% Alipay, 38.8% Tenpay, 5.6% others. Do not replace the current figure with a falsely precise single estimate.
- **Recommended replacement/correction 1.** Never quote `5.pdf` page 1’s reversed “Tenpay 53%, Alipay 39%” sentence; use the page-2 chart allocation and flag the typo in data-quality notes.
- **Recommended replacement/correction 2.** Do not call any 2025E value “Bloomberg consensus.” Use “Bloomberg Intelligence scenario published in 2019/2021.”
- **Recommended caveat 1.** Treat the 14 October 2026 footer as export metadata only. The information set is 2019 or March 2021.
- **Recommended caveat 2.** Keep stocks, flows and shares separate: loan balance, wealth assets and funding mix are stocks; revenue, net fees and payment-clearing value are period flows; market share is a ratio whose value/volume basis is unspecified.
- **Recommended caveat 3.** Classify WeBank and MYbank as licensed digital banks. Their deposits, LDR, NIM and balance-sheet loans are not comparable to non-bank loan facilitators’ originations or enabled balances.
- **Recommended caveat 4.** Treat “users/borrowers” and “funding partners” as heterogeneous headline metrics. They do not resolve MAU, active-borrower, funded-account, exposure concentration or risk-bearing questions.
- **Investment implication.** The strongest incremental evidence is for the licensed-hybrid thesis. WeBank’s dramatic deposit mobilisation and MYbank’s loan execution show that platform ecosystems can produce real banking franchises, while the fee forecasts and partner counts show that those franchises still depend on cross-selling and financial-institution networks. Pure facilitators remain more exposed to rules that constrain funding partners and required capital.

### Gaps

- Post-2020 private-company disclosure remains the largest gap: Ant, JD Technology, WeBank and MYbank still need primary annual-report or shareholder-filing histories through 2025/2026.
- Provider-level payment shares still need explicit denominator metadata: transaction value versus count, third-party mobile versus all mobile, merchant versus P2P, domestic versus cross-border, and inclusion of UnionPay/QuickPass.
- No PDF data resolve current UnionPay versus NetsUnion volume/value, Ant consumer-credit risk capital, Huabei/Jiebei balance, MYbank retained versus partner-funded credit, WeBank syndicated balance, or the listed facilitators’ outstanding/risk-bearing splits.
- Realised 2025 net fees for WeBank/MYbank are not present, so the BI fee forecast cannot yet be scored.
- The old BI reports are insufficient for valuation work. A separate Bloomberg export or public-market dataset is still required for historical/current P/E, P/B, EV/revenue, dividend yield and FY2026E-28E consensus.
